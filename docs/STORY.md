# From questions to working tools

I'm Skandar, a self-taught software builder in the Netherlands. I learn through projects: getting something running, understanding why it fails, and refining it until the result makes sense.

My interests reach beyond software. Physics and space raise questions about underlying mechanisms. History and institutions raise questions about incentives, decisions and how explanations are constructed. Building gives me a way to turn some of that curiosity into something I can inspect and use.

## A research question becomes an archive

In March 2026, a conversation about the film *Cube* developed into broader questions about systems, institutions and AI's limits. It also led to an extended collection of Arabic documentary material, including EFNETWORK / Andoria.

I worked through collecting subtitles and merging them into a searchable corpus. The project contains **98 subtitle files** and a Python merger. A later dashboard organizes transcripts alongside timelines, maps and connections.

The achievement here is the collection and the tooling. The claims in a source still need their own evidence. Keeping material searchable makes it easier to return to the actual words and compare explanations.

## A visual problem becomes a structural one

While developing a project roadmap, the connecting curve kept missing its dot centers. Repeated visual fixes were not settling the relationship.

I proposed changing the dot positions to agree with the curve. The resulting approach coordinates the positions and constructs a path through them.

That episode captures a habit I bring to software: when two parts keep disagreeing, look for the constraint they should share. I direct and refine AI-assisted development through requirements, debugging, visual review and checks of the result.

## Spare hardware becomes deployment infrastructure

The phone-hosting work behind DroidDeploy grew into **anyDeploy**: a desktop app for deploying projects to hardware already available, including Android, Raspberry Pi, Linux and Windows targets.

That work reaches beyond a deploy button. It involves setup, connections, state, packaging and updates. Public Windows and Linux release artifacts are available.

[Explore anyDeploy](https://github.com/JPEG111/anydeploy-releases)

## A desktop feature becomes a lifecycle

**Orbs** is Windows tooling for supported Discord game activity and Quests, with a queue and an English / Egyptian Arabic interface.

Much of the engineering is in the surrounding behavior: reading client state, handling interruptions, retaining a queue, installing updates and preserving preferences. Dated verification checkpoints include source/compiled tests, owned native/browser fixtures and selected live-client paths.

[Explore Orbs](https://github.com/JPEG111/orbs-release) · [Achievement notes](ACHIEVEMENTS.md)

## Remembering becomes a workflow question

Long AI-assisted tasks repeatedly bring me back to continuity. A system can save a decision and still fail to retrieve it, or retrieve it without applying it.

In October 2026, I explored a planning system with connected decisions, source evidence and prerequisite checks. My Lego-machine analogy is that the system should make a missing piece visible before allowing the next step.

Then comes the next question: does recording more create clutter, stale information and pressure on the context window? That is part of what I want to test. Strategy OS is an architectural direction, while OmniAgent has separate local implementation and acceptance work.

## Where it goes next

I want clearer setup journeys, tools that retain the decisions behind their actions, and experiments that another run can reproduce.

The next useful step is a bounded workflow with an observable result. [The roadmap](ROADMAP.md) defines those milestones.
